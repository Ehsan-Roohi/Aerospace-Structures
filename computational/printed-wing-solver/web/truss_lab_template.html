How to build and analyse a truss
  1. Start empty. Choose Add node and click the drawing, or type coordinates on the left. Coordinates and members use mm; E uses MPa; A uses mm²; forces use N.
  2. Choose Add member and click two nodes. Set E and A before adding; each member can later have its own material and area.
  3. Choose Select, then a node. Assign pin (restrains x and y), roller Y (restrains y only), roller X (restrains x only), or free. Enter Fx and Fy; +x is right, +y is up. Loads act only at joints.
  4. Solve. Blue members are in tension; orange members are in compression. The dashed shape is magnified and its scale is stated. Inspect numerical member forces, stresses, displacements and reactions.
  5. Click a node or member to edit. Delete removes the selection; deleting a node also removes connected members. Delete in a text field edits text, not geometry. Clear all asks for confirmation. Edits hide old results until solved again.

Model: 2-D pin-jointed straight axial bars, linear isotropic elasticity, small displacements, no member bending or automatic self-weight. Crossings are not joints unless explicitly connected through a node. No cable slackening, compression buckling, joint failure, prestress, or full-aircraft certification. A wire represented as a bar can incorrectly carry compression; tension-only wire analysis is not supported.

The optional aircraft-inspired example is a generic braced bay, not a reconstruction or validated model of the Wright Flyer. A real aircraft requires credible geometry, joints, loads and material data; many aircraft members carry bending as well as axial load.
