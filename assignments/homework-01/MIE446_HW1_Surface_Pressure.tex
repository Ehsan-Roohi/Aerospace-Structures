\section*{Purpose}
In this homework you will compute the two-dimensional pressure force produced by a NACA 2412 airfoil. Question 1 uses continuous geometry and pressure functions. Question 2 uses 101 supplied stations and straight-panel sums. No derivation of the pressure-force formulas is required.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.94\textwidth}{\textbf{Model statement.} The supplied pressure distribution is a calibrated instructional surrogate for attached flow over a NACA 2412 section at $\alpha=5^{\circ}$. It is designed to reproduce a physically reasonable suction peak and a lift coefficient near the historical NACA 2412 trend. It is \emph{not} presented as pressure-tap measurement data or as a universal pressure law.}}
\end{center}

Let $\X=x/c$ and $\overline y=y/c$. The $x$-axis points from the leading edge toward the trailing edge; the positive $y$-axis points upward. Pressure coefficient is
\begin{equation}
C_p=\frac{p-p_\infty}{q_\infty}.
\end{equation}
The pressure resultant is first resolved into a chord-normal coefficient $C_N$ and a chord-axial coefficient $C_A$. Positive $C_A$ points from leading edge to trailing edge. Lift and pressure drag are then obtained by rotation into wind axes:
\begin{align}
C_L &= C_N\cos\alpha-C_A\sin\alpha,\\
C_{D,p} &= C_N\sin\alpha+C_A\cos\alpha.
\end{align}

Only pressure forces are included. Therefore $C_{D,p}$ is \textbf{pressure drag}, not total viscous drag; skin-friction drag cannot be recovered from the supplied pressure data.

\section*{NACA 2412 geometry used in both questions}
For NACA 2412,
\[
m=0.02,\qquad p=0.40,\qquad t=0.12.
\]
The nondimensional mean camber line is
\begin{equation}
\Yc(\X)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{m}{p^2}\left(2p\X-\X^2\right), & 0\le \X\le p,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{m}{(1-p)^2}\left[(1-2p)+2p\X-\X^2\right], & p<\X\le1,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and the closed-trailing-edge thickness distribution is
\begin{equation}
\Yt(\X)=5t\left(0.2969\sqrt{\X}-0.1260\X-0.3516\X^2+0.2843\X^3-0.1036\X^4\right).
\end{equation}
Use the common-$x$ approximation
\begin{equation}
\Yu=\Yc+\Yt,\qquad \Yl=\Yc-\Yt.
\end{equation}
The slopes needed in the force integral are
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\Yu}{d\X}=\frac{d\Yc}{d\X}+\frac{d\Yt}{d\X},\qquad
\frac{d\Yl}{d\X}=\frac{d\Yc}{d\X}-\frac{d\Yt}{d\X},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\Yc}{d\X}=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2m}{p^2}(p-\X), & 0\le \X\le p,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{2m}{(1-p)^2}(p-\X), & p<\X\le1,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\Yt}{d\X}=5t\left(\frac{0.2969}{2\sqrt{\X}}-0.1260-0.7032\X+0.8529\X^2-0.4144\X^3\right).
\end{equation}

\section*{Question 1 — Continuous surface integration \hfill [45 points]}
Use this smooth loading model on $0\le\X\le1$:
\begin{align}
C_0(\X) &= (1-\X)\left[e^{-\X/0.010}+0.20\left(1-e^{-\X/0.020}\right)\right],\\
\Delta C_p(\X) &= 0.721896\,\frac{\left(1-e^{-\X/0.010}\right)(1-\X)}{\sqrt{\X+0.020}},\\
\Cpu(\X) &= C_0(\X)-0.80\,\Delta C_p(\X),\\
\Cpl(\X) &= C_0(\X)+0.20\,\Delta C_p(\X).
\end{align}
If needed, $p_u=p_\infty+q_\infty\Cpu$ and $p_l=p_\infty+q_\infty\Cpl$.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{1\alph*.}]
\item Decode the four digits of NACA 2412. Calculate $q_\infty$ and state its units.
\item Plot $\Yu$ and $\Yl$ on equal axes. Separately plot $\Cpu$ and $\Cpl$ versus $\X$ with an inverted $C_p$ axis. Label the leading-edge suction region and the pressure difference producing positive lift.
\item Numerically evaluate the following \emph{supplied} pressure-force integrals. No proof or derivation is required. Document your numerical method and convergence.
\begin{align}
C_N &= \int_0^1(\Cpl-\Cpu)\,d\X,\\
C_A &= \int_0^1\left(\Cpu\frac{d\Yu}{d\X}-\Cpl\frac{d\Yl}{d\X}\right)d\X.
\end{align}
\item Rotate $C_N$ and $C_A$ to obtain $C_L$ and $C_{D,p}$, then calculate the forces per unit span:
\[
L'=q_\infty c C_L,\qquad D_p'=q_\infty c C_{D,p}.
\]
Report coefficients to four significant figures and forces with units. Do not call pressure drag total drag.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Numerical integration guide for 1c}
The thickness slope contains $1/\sqrt{\X}$ and is undefined at $\X=0$, although its integral is finite. Do not evaluate the raw slope at that endpoint. The recommended approach is $\X=s^2$, $d\X=2s\,ds$, $0\le s\le1$. This concentrates stations near the leading edge. Define
\begin{align}
g_N(s)&=2s\left[\Cpl(s^2)-\Cpu(s^2)\right],\\
g_A(s)&=2s\left[\Cpu(s^2)\Yu'(s^2)-\Cpl(s^2)\Yl'(s^2)\right].
\end{align}
A prime denotes $d\overline y/d\X$. At $s=0$, use the finite limits $g_N(0)=0$ and $g_A(0)=10t(0.2969)=0.35628$; do not calculate $0\times\infty$.

For either $g$, use $s_j=j/N$ and the composite trapezoidal rule:
\[
\int_0^1g(s)\,ds\ \approx\ \frac1N\left[\frac12g(0)+
\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}g\!\left(\frac jN\right)+\frac12g(1)\right].
\]
Calculate once with $N=1000$ and again with $N=2000$. Report both $C_N$ and $C_A$ at each resolution and the absolute differences; differences below $10^{-4}$ are adequate.

\textbf{Adaptive quadrature} means software estimates integration error and subdivides intervals where the function changes rapidly. You need not implement it. If you use a library such as \texttt{scipy.integrate.quad}, apply it to $g_N$ and $g_A$ on $[0,1]$, state the tolerance, and repeat with a tighter tolerance.

\section*{Question 2 — Tabulated data and panel sums \hfill [45 points]}
Use the companion workbook \texttt{MIE446\_HW1\_NACA2412\_Data.xlsx}. The \texttt{Data} sheet supplies 101 stations from $\X=0$ to 1 with $\X$, $\Yu$, $\Yl$, $p_u$, and $p_l$. Preserve all supplied data columns. Consecutive stations define 100 straight panels; panel $i$ connects stations $i$ and $i+1$.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{2\alph*.}]
\item Add columns for $C_{p,u,i}$ and $C_{p,l,i}$ calculated from the dimensional pressures and the supplied $p_\infty$ and $q_\infty$.
\item For every panel, calculate mean upper/lower $C_p$, the changes in $\X$, $\Yu$, and $\Yl$, and the direct normal/axial force contributions below. Keep one visible row per panel. No finite-difference slopes are needed.
\begin{align}
\overline C_{p,u,i}&=\frac{C_{p,u,i}+C_{p,u,i+1}}2,\qquad
\Delta\X_i=\X_{i+1}-\X_i,\\
\Delta C_{N,i}&=\frac{(C_{p,l,i}-C_{p,u,i})+(C_{p,l,i+1}-C_{p,u,i+1})}{2}\,\Delta\X_i,\\
\Delta C_{A,i}&=\overline C_{p,u,i}(\overline y_{u,i+1}-\overline y_{u,i})
-\overline C_{p,l,i}(\overline y_{l,i+1}-\overline y_{l,i}).
\end{align}
Use the analogous mean formula for $\overline C_{p,l,i}$.
\item Sum the 100 panel contributions for $C_N$ and $C_A$:
\[
C_N\approx\sum_{i=1}^{100}\Delta C_{N,i},\qquad
C_A\approx\sum_{i=1}^{100}\Delta C_{A,i}.
\]
Rotate to $C_L$ and $C_{D,p}$ and calculate $L'$ and $D_p'$. Compare $C_L$ and $C_{D,p}$ with Question 1 using
\[
\text{Percentage difference (\%)}=
100\left|\frac{\text{panel result}-\text{continuous result}}
{\text{continuous result}}\right|.
\]
\item In two or three sentences, compare the lift and pressure-drag differences. Pressure drag is more sensitive to panel resolution when its wind-axis terms partly cancel. Do not infer skin-friction drag from these data.
\end{enumerate}
