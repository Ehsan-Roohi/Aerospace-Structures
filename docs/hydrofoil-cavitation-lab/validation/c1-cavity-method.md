# Axisymmetric disk cavity: C1 curved sheet with least-squares Neumann collocation

Version 0.3.0-c1-research of the developed-cavity solver (`axisymmetric-cavity-c1.js`) is an independent accuracy repair of the continuous linear nodal vortex formulation of version 0.2.1 (`axisymmetric-cavity-nodal.js`). The physical model, the node placement, the unknowns, the dynamic rows, the Newton iteration, the drag normalization and every accuracy gate are unchanged. What changes is the representation of the sheet between the nodes and the way the Neumann condition is imposed. This note records the review of the 0.2.1 field defects, the repaired formulation, and the numerical evidence. It is numerical verification only. No experimental validation is performed or claimed, and no literature number is counted as a passed benchmark.

## 1. Review: why the polygon could not pass the nine-fraction audit

Version 0.2.1 represents the meridian as straight conical facets between nodes with a continuous, piecewise linear vortex strength. The stable kernel of that version removed cancellation in the ring-minus-planar remainder and drove the Newton residual to 7.1e-15 (64 cavity panels) and 1.7e-13 (256 cavity panels). The direct-field audit on the nine fractions 0.02 to 0.98 of every panel nevertheless gave maximum defects of 0.01260 U (normal velocity) and 0.04152 Cp (dynamic condition) at 256 cavity panels, and quadrature refinement changed nothing at the 1e-10 level. That behaviour is a property of the representation, not of the arithmetic.

On a polygon with turning angle Δθ at a vertex and continuous strength γ, the exact velocity of the sheet is logarithmically singular at the vertex. Summing the analytic straight-panel integrals of the two panels meeting at a vertex, the logarithmic terms cancel along the common normal only to O(Δθ²), while the tangential component keeps a term (γ/2π) sin Δθ · log(ρ/h) where ρ is the distance to the vertex. The dynamic defect therefore grows without bound as a sample approaches a node, with amplitude proportional to the local turning angle. Near the rim of the disk the free surface turns through 90 degrees over a short arclength, so the turning angles of the first panels are the largest (about 0.06 rad per panel at 256 cavity panels); the worst samples of the 0.2.1 audit sit exactly there (figure `c1-cavity-profiles`). Because the amplitude only decreases like the panel size, and the logarithm is unbounded, a sample at 2 percent of a panel is worse than one at 10 percent at every resolution. This is why the five-fraction audit passed at 256 cavity panels and the nine-fraction audit did not. The sampled normal velocity shares the same mechanism through the mismatch between the collocation points (midpoints) and the inter-node variation of the facet field. No change of thresholds, no removal of endpoint panels and no relabelling of the reconstructed trace can repair this; the geometry has to become at least C1 and the field has to be evaluated on the curved surface.

## 2. Repaired formulation

Unbounded steady incompressible potential flow past a sharp disk of radius R at x = 0 with a Riabouchinsky image disk at x = L (L/R is the full separation); free surface from rim to image rim; interior at rest; vortex sheet of strength γ per unit meridian length with the liquid on the left of the direction axis, rim, image rim, axis.

Geometry. The cavity nodes are the 0.2.1 nodes: a reference curve r = R + (Rm − R) (4x(L − x)/L²)^{2/3} carrying the sharp-edge exponent, sampled by cosine in reference arclength, displaced along the reference normals by the unknowns d₁ … d_{Nc/2} with fore-aft symmetry. Node k therefore sits at the graded parameter τ = πk/Nc. Between nodes the meridian is a C1 cubic Hermite curve in τ with fourth-order slope estimates; the parity of the sharp-edge solution, x odd and r even in τ about each rim, supplies the stencil extension and makes the slope exactly zero at the rims. The two rim panels use the exact parity form x = B w³ + E w⁵, r − R = A w² + C w⁴ (w measured from the rim), so the free surface leaves the rim tangent to the face with x ∝ (r − R)^{3/2}, as the local free-streamline solution requires, and the curvature singularity κ ∝ s^{−1/2} is analytic in τ. The faces are exact, parametrized by r = sin φ, whose speed also vanishes at the rim. No kink exists anywhere on the meridian.

Sheet strength. The nodal values γ_k are the same unknowns as before. Between nodes γ is a C2 cubic spline in the piece parameter (φ on the faces, τ on the free surface). The end conditions are those of the local edge solution, for which q = qc(1 − a√s + …) on the wetted face and θ = a√s + … on the free surface (the mixed problem for log(w/qc) in the half plane): γ odd at the stagnation axis (natural spline), free (not-a-knot) at the rim on the face, zero slope at the rim and at the mid-plane on the free surface. The nodal reconstruction −γ_k remains the trace; it is never relabelled as the exterior speed.

Kernels. Velocities come from the elliptic-integral vortex-ring kernels. For a field point on or near a panel the planar point-vortex kernel is subtracted on the tangent line through the nearest point of the curve, parametrized by the true arclength σ(z) of the panel, so that only curvature terms and the ring logarithm remain in the numerical remainder. The density on the tangent segment is a cubic in σ that matches value and slope at the foot and the values at two far points; its planar integral, the Cauchy principal value and the side jump are analytic (cubic-density planar segment). The ring-minus-planar remainder is evaluated without cancellation (rationalized √D − S and the DLMF 19.12 expansion of E − 1, which agrees to 4e-16 with the independently derived 0.2.1 `vortexRemainder`), position differences are evaluated by exact algebraic factorization, and the quadrature nodes cluster as u³ toward the foot, which makes the endpoint behaviour u² log u and converges the discrete solution to 3e-10 in σ with 24 points per side. Toward a rim end, where the parametrization speed vanishes and the kernel is already regular, no subtraction is used.

Neumann condition. The normal velocity is imposed in weighted least squares at mColl = 3 Gauss points per panel of the symmetric half, with meridian arclength weights. This is a numerical least-squares discretization. The published current mesh study fixes mColl=3; its successful exact-solution and full-field controls support those recorded cases, not a universal stability theorem. The free-surface Newton iteration on (d, qc) with the nodal dynamic rows is unchanged; its finite-difference Jacobian uses a first-order least-squares correction with the factored normal matrix, which reproduces full re-solves to O(1e-7) relative and keeps the quadratic convergence.

Drag. CD = 2 ∫₀¹ (qc² − V²) r dr on the physical front face, normalized by ½ρU²πR², with V the trace; the same integral evaluated with the directly computed exterior speed is reported as `CD_directField`. The image-disk force is not counted.

## 3. Evidence

All checks are in `c1-cavity-verify.cjs` and recorded in `c1-cavity-verification.json` (26 checks, status PASS_NUMERICAL_VERIFICATION_ONLY). Kernel level: curved-panel influence against brute-force three-dimensional Biot–Savart integration over the frustum of revolution at eight configurations including points 1e-3 and 1e-4 from the rim panels and near a node (maximum relative error 5.5e-9); the on-surface exterior limit is the limit of off-surface values at seven locations including both rims, with ratio 0.50 per halving of the offset; the analytic cubic-density segment matches 64-point Gauss to 1e-16 and carries the exact jump; the spline operator reproduces cubics exactly.

Exact solution. Uniform flow past the unit sphere (surface speed 1.5 sin θ, exact exterior field). With the same curved Hermite geometry, spline density and least-squares collocation, the maximum surface normal velocity at the nine fractions is 4.3e-5 (N = 16), 5.4e-6 (32), 6.8e-7 (64), 8.6e-8 (128), observed order 3.0; the nodal γ error converges at order 4 to 1.4e-9; exterior and interior field points converge at order 4 to 9e-10 and 4e-10. With exact circular arcs instead of Hermite curves the surface defect reaches 3.5e-10 at N = 128 at order 4, which identifies the O(h²) curvature jumps of the C1 geometry as the remaining limiter.

Cross-implementation. `reference/c1-cavity/python/axi_c1.py` implements the same formulation independently: SciPy elliptic integrals instead of the arithmetic-geometric mean, a SciPy `CubicSpline` slope operator, singular-value least squares instead of Householder, a finite-difference Jacobian from full re-assembly and re-solve instead of the incremental correction, different quadrature counts. Fresh SciPy reruns at Nc32 and Nc64 agree with the current browser equation implementation to at most 1.3e-14 in σ, Rmax and CD. Every nodal strength and node is also compared in `c1-reviewed-verification.json`; both implementations are numerical solutions of the same model, not experimental data.

## 4. What is not established

Field accuracy is numerically qualified by the unchanged gates and by the exact-solution, brute-force and cross-implementation controls above. Nothing here is experimental validation: no matched tunnel experiment, no blockage, viscosity, nuclei, natural detachment, re-entrant jet, unsteadiness or structural coupling. The Riabouchinsky image is a closure model; its σ(L/R) relation and the front-face pressure are model results. Literature comparisons (for example Brennen 1969, which solved the same Riabouchinsky disk model numerically, and the classical disk drag correlations) are external numerical references and are to be reported separately from measurements; experimental sphere or tunnel markers must not be read as matched unbounded-disk data. The C1 geometry limits the surface-defect convergence to third order; a C2 quintic Hermite meridian with fourth-order curvature estimates is the natural next step if a fourth-order field is wanted, and the Python twin should be rerun whenever the formulation changes.
