Constrained Bayesian Optimization for Expensive Solves

constrained-bo 0.1.1
Scope. When one exact structural solve is expensive (for example the coupled aerodynamic and structural solve of a whole wing), a grid search is unaffordable. This page shows how a Gaussian process (GP) surrogate plus a constrained expected-improvement acquisition chooses the next design to evaluate, how the surrogate's uncertainty is displayed honestly, and why the recommendation is always a design that was actually evaluated and then re-solved exactly, never a surrogate estimate. The demonstration solvers are deliberately cheap closed forms: an Euler-Bernoulli beam (linear elastic, small deflection, four classical load cases) and a 2-D linear elastic pin-jointed Warren truss with an Euler buckling screen (effective length factor K = 1, solid square section assumed). The optional solve delay is a demonstration device that only emulates an expensive solver. No certified strengths, no stability or fatigue claims; independent of the Wing AeroStruct solver.

How it works

The optimizer, step by step
  1. Initial design. A seeded Latin hypercube sample (LHS) of 2n + 2 designs is solved exactly (n is the number of design variables).
  2. Surrogates. One Gaussian process (GP) is fitted to the mass and one to each constraint value g_j (feasible means g_j <= 0). Hyperparameters are refitted after every evaluation.
  3. Acquisition. Constrained expected improvement (Gardner et al. 2014): EI(x) times the product over constraints of P(g_j(x) <= 0). EI is measured against the best feasible design evaluated so far. While no feasible design exists, the probability of feasibility alone is maximized.
  4. Maximization. 400 quasi-random Halton candidates are scored, the best three are polished with the Nelder-Mead simplex method, and the result is clamped to the variable bounds. A point that would repeat an evaluated design is replaced by the candidate where the surrogate is least certain.
  5. Exact evaluation. The chosen design is solved exactly and added to the data. Steps 2 to 5 repeat until the budget is spent.
  6. Recommendation. The best evaluated feasible design is re-solved from scratch and every constraint is re-checked. That verified result is what you see.
Why the answer never comes from the surrogate
The GP mean is an interpolation of a handful of solves. It can be wrong by several times its own standard deviation where data are sparse, and its feasibility probability is a model statement, not a check. This page therefore uses the surrogate only to decide where to look next. Every number in the key results comes from the exact solver, and the recommendation carries the badge "Checked by fresh model solve" only after a fresh solve of the recommended design passes all constraints. The honesty panel below shows the GP's own estimate at the recommendation with its ±2 sigma range so that you can judge how much (or how little) the surrogate should be trusted.
The Gaussian process in one paragraph
Inputs are scaled to the unit box and outputs are standardized. The kernel is Matern 5/2 with one length scale per input (automatic relevance determination, ARD) and a signal variance; the mean is the sample mean; the diagonal receives a nugget of 1e-6 plus a small noise term of 1e-4 (standardized units). Hyperparameters maximize the log marginal likelihood on a grid of 5 length scales per dimension and 5 signal variances (full grid for two variables, coordinate-wise search for three). Constraint GPs are fitted to the signed logarithm sign(g) ln(1 + |g|) because utilizations span orders of magnitude across the design box; the transform is monotone and keeps zero fixed, so P(g <= 0) is unchanged. Factorizations use Cholesky with a jitter fallback.
The demonstration solvers

Beam (Euler-Bernoulli, linear elastic, small deflection): cantilever with tip load, M = P L and v = P L^3 / (3 E I); cantilever with uniform load, M = w L^2 / 2 and v = w L^4 / (8 E I); simply supported with midspan load, M = P L / 4 and v = P L^3 / (48 E I); simply supported with uniform load, M = w L^2 / 8 and v = 5 w L^4 / (384 E I). Constraints: U_stress = |M| c FS / (I sigma_allow) <= 1, U_defl = v / (L / k) <= 1, and a minimum feature thickness t_min / t_section <= 1. Objective: mass = A L rho / 1000 in grams. For tubes a wall at or beyond the radius (2 t >= D) is treated as the solid-bar limit, so D > 2 t acts as a bound.

Warren truss (2-D pin-jointed, linear elastic, direct stiffness): chord area, diagonal area and height h (all top-chord nodes move together) are the variables. Member force N = (E A / L) times the axial elongation. Tension: U = |N| FS / (A sigma_allow). Compression: U = max(stress utilization, |N| FS / P_cr) with P_cr = pi^2 E I / (K L)^2, I = A^2 / 12 (solid square) and K = 1. This buckling check is a screen: the effective length and the section shape are assumed, not analysed. Midspan deflection is limited to span / 250 by default. A singular stiffness matrix (mechanism) is reported as an unstable structure and counted as a large constraint violation.

Same budget cap comparison and reference optimum
With a budget of B exact solves the uniform grid uses floor(sqrt(B))^2 points for two variables and floor(B^(1/3))^3 for three, placed on the bounds inclusive. The random search draws B designs uniformly with the same seed. The reference optimum is computed outside the budget with the cheap exact solver: for the beam, a fine 1-D scan over the free section variable with bisection on the other (every constraint is monotone in it); for the rectangular section the classical closed form (width at its lower bound or depth at its upper bound, active constraint solved explicitly) is shown as well. For the truss, a 1-D scan over h uses static determinacy (member forces do not depend on the areas) and solves the deflection constraint in closed form. All references are re-solved exactly before they are displayed.
Using the engine with your own expensive solver
The module exports optimize({ n, lower, upper, evaluate(x) -> { f, g: [] }, budget, seed }, onProgress) where evaluate is pure and synchronous and g holds the constraint values (feasible when all are <= 0). The worker protocol (messages run, cancel, progress, done) is documented in the README so another app such as Wing AeroStruct can plug in its solver.
Limitations
Two and three design variables only in this demonstration; the GP grid search for hyperparameters is coarse by design; the acquisition maximizer is a heuristic (quasi-random candidates plus local polishing) and can miss a narrow peak; results are deterministic for a seed but different seeds give different paths. The demonstration solvers are simplified models with educational material cards and are not design allowables.
