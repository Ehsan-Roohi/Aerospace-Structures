Numerical evidence and open accuracy gates

Research model: physical cavitation validation has not passed. Endpoint and trailing-edge numerical checks are available; the default 2D branch fails an additional wetted-pressure consistency screen. A matched experimental benchmark and an admitted independent free-boundary solution are still required. These results are not a three-dimensional cavitating-wing analysis.

Current solver identifies the implementation used for each recorded test. The 2D path uses smooth-contact recovery selection plus velocity Kutta; its core equations are unchanged from 0.3.2. The fully wetted 3D body path uses source rings. The developed-disk path now uses continuous linear-vortex rings and reports reconstructed and direct-field pressures separately. “Independent Hess–Smith” is a separate 2D velocity-source/vortex discretization; an “exact” curve is an analytical solution. Neither means experimental data.

What is verified, and what is not?

A small equation residual means that the discrete equations were solved. Agreement with an independent numerical method is verification. Comparison with matched measurements is experimental validation. These are different checks.

Disk cavity: comparison with matched published theory

The current 0.2.1 stable-kernel axisymmetric solver is compared with the theoretical disk pressure profiles at σ = 0.3 and 0.7 in Brennen (1969), p.680, Figure 3 (author's source record). This is a published numerical-reference comparison, not experimental validation. Six fresh nonlinear solves use the same disk and Riabouchinsky image closure. For each mesh, the full disk-to-image separation L/R is solved until the computed σ matches the reference within 5 × 10−8; no pressure-profile parameters are fitted.

New: stable axisymmetric kernel and declared surface traces

Axisymmetric cavity version 0.2.1 removes cancellation between the elliptic ring kernel and its planar singular part. An independent 400-digit calculation of the original expressions checks the rearrangement. A declared boundary-panel trace now selects the mathematical liquid/interior limit despite coordinate roundoff; off-surface declarations and endpoints are refused. Neither pressure targets nor physical coefficients are fitted.

At 64 cavity panels the same Newton equations now reach a residual of 7.11 × 10−15, below the unchanged 10−10 tolerance; the previous kernel stalled at 2.51 × 10−10. The audit now retains nine fractions of every panel: 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9, 0.95 and 0.98. A pass on the earlier five-fraction grid is not interpreted as a continuous-boundary pass.

Field accuracy remains unqualified. At 256 cavity panels the expanded-grid maximum normal and pressure residuals are 0.01260 U and 0.04152 Cp, above the unchanged 0.01 U and 0.02 Cp limits. This is a numerical repair, not completed physical or experimental validation. The two-dimensional wetted-pressure defect also remains unresolved.

True three-dimensional axisymmetric flow

Fully wetted flow and an inception indicator only. The sphere and prolate spheroid are surfaces of revolution integrated as three-dimensional source rings. Their computed pressure is compared with independent analytical potential-flow solutions; the exact values do not enter the boundary solve. This fully wetted mode does not itself solve a developed cavity; the separate research module above does. The exact curves are evaluated at the corresponding analytic meridional midpoint parameter; the numerical body uses conical bands.

The Bernoulli criterion compares σ=(p∞−pv)/(½ρU²) with −min Cp of the wet solution. A wet-flow pressure below vapour pressure indicates that this wet solution is no longer physically sufficient there; it is not relabelled as a solved cavity. Viscosity, nuclei and tunnel effects are not included. Equations, signs, self-panel quadrature and limitations.

The axisymmetric extension is informed by Alavi & Roohi, Cavitation review Part I (2026). Its disk/sphere free-cavity comparisons require matched detachment, closure and tunnel conditions; they are not used as experimental validation of this wet-flow module.

2D symmetric-cavity numerical repair

What changed

The old fixed recovery attenuation could close the cavity height while leaving a growing endpoint corner. The current model solves attenuation A with a physical-gap tangent-contact equation. An independent wake-strength unknown and a velocity Kutta row also replace the default potential-jump approximation. Historical fixed-A/Morino calculations remain available for old case files.
