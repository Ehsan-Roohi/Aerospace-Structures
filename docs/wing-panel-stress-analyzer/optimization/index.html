What this is. Ten small, self-contained pages that take the beam, truss and wing-box problems students already solve in MIE 446 and add the design-optimization layer: where does your design sit in the design space, which constraint governs, what is the lightest design that still passes, and how do classical optimization methods (fully stressed design, optimality criteria, linear programming, Nelder-Mead, CMA-ES, NSGA-II, Bayesian optimization) actually work. What it is not. Every page uses its own simplified linear-elastic model stated at the top of the page; nothing here is a certified strength or stability analysis, and nothing here reads or replaces the Wing AeroStruct solver. Every recommended design is re-solved from scratch with the exact solver of its page before it is shown, Final candidate metrics are recomputed with that model; surrogate curves are explicitly approximate.

Suggested order for students

  1. Solve the beam or truss by hand and in the Beam or Truss Lab page first. Write down your section or member sizes.
  2. Open the matching lab page, type your design as "your design", and read the governing constraint and the mass saving the optimizer finds.
  3. Only then look at how the optimizer got there (the "How it works" block on every page) and at the classical result it reproduces.

Conventions shared by all pages

Units: mm, N, MPa, g. Utilization U is demand divided by capacity with the factor of safety included, so every check passes when U is at most 1. Green means feasible, amber means active or within ten percent of the limit, red means violated. "Screen" marks an indicative check with assumed boundary conditions, for example the Euler buckling estimate with an assumed effective length. Material cards are representative educational values; printed parts should use measured coupon data. Source files, tests and release checksums are in the course repository under docs/wing-panel-stress-analyzer/optimization/.
