How to build and analyse a truss
  1. Start empty. Choose + Node and click the drawing, or type coordinates on the left. Choose grid spacing; alignment snaps x/y to an existing node within 10 drawing pixels. The cursor guides show the exact proposed coordinates. Both axes have the same physical scale. Coordinates use mm; E uses MPa; A uses mm²; forces use N.
  2. Set new member type: Bar or Wire (tension only), E and A. Choose + Member and click two nodes. A crossing is not a joint unless connected explicitly through a node. Click a member or node in Select mode to open its editable properties window. Drag a node to move it with snapping; Delete removes a selected item. Undo/Redo restores model edits, deletions, loads and supports (up to 100 saved states); keyboard Ctrl/Cmd+Z and Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z work outside text fields.
  3. Use the PLACE icons above the drawing, then click a node: pin restrains x and y, roller Y restrains y only, roller X restrains x only. An angled roller restrains its normal and allows sliding along its surface. Enter the surface angle or drag its green rotation handle in Select mode. Pin rotation changes its depiction only; it still restrains both translations. Remove a restraint by selecting its symbol and pressing Delete, or set the node support to Free.
  4. Choose ↓ Point force above the drawing, click a node, enter direction and magnitude in the closable form below the model, and Apply. No load is inserted automatically. +x is right, +y is up. A new force replaces that node's previous resultant; use Custom Fx/Fy for combined forces. A couple cannot act on an ideal pin joint: this axial model has no rotational DOF. For distributed loading choose Distributed → joints, specify an ordered path and signed q₀/q₁ in N/mm. It assumes an external collector transfers loads to joints; it does not solve member bending.
  5. Solve. Blue is tension; orange is compression. Wires carry tension only; slack wires have zero force and are faint/dashed. The dashed deformed shape is amplified, with its scale stated. Edits invalidate old results. A mechanism or failed cable iteration gives no accepted result.
  6. For bar buckling screening, enter weakest-axis Imin and effective length factor K. Pcr = π² E Imin / (K L)². L is the joint-to-joint member length; split members or choose a justified K for the actual unbraced length. Blank I means NOT CHECKED. Euler assumes a straight, slender, elastic column; local buckling, yielding, joints and global instability require separate checks. Exceeding Pcr does not produce a post-buckling deformation here.
  7. Select a force arrow, support symbol, member or node and press Delete. Deleting a node also deletes its connected members. Delete inside a text field edits text. Clear all forces preserves geometry; Clear all removes the model after confirmation; Undo can restore it.

How distributed loading reaches a truss

Wing pressure is first carried by covering, ribs, spars and connections. The axial truss receives their forces at its joints. This tool represents a specified external load-collection path, not an automatic pressure-to-wing structural model. For each straight path interval of length ℓ with intensities qₐ and qᵦ, the equivalent endpoint forces in the entered direction are Fₐ=ℓ(2qₐ+qᵦ)/6 and Fᵦ=ℓ(qₐ+2qᵦ)/6. They preserve resultant force and moment; contributions from adjacent intervals add at a shared joint. Uniform loading gives half an interval’s load at each end. For equally spaced joints an interior joint therefore receives twice an end joint’s share. A flexible collector can distribute loads differently: use a beam/frame model for its actual reactions when that matters. Results show manual, transferred and total nodal loads separately, without automatic double counting.

Model: 2-D pin-jointed axial members with small displacements. Wire force N = max(0, E A ΔL / L), with zero initial slack and no pretension; this is not a sagging catenary or geometrically nonlinear cable model. A shortened wire carries zero force, not compression. Joint separation change may be negative for a slack wire while its elastic extension is zero. At zero force the wire is at the taut/slack boundary; a different load may change stability. The active-set solver has a finite iteration limit and a bounded exhaustive fallback for at most 10 wires; lack of convergence is reported rather than stabilized artificially. No member bending, automatic self-weight, prestress, large-motion cable action, joint failure or full-aircraft certification.

The optional braced bay is generic, not a reconstruction or validated model of the Wright Flyer. A real aircraft needs credible geometry, joints, wire tensions, loads, bracing and material data.

References: Tension-only tie behaviour (Oasys); Euler buckling of an ideal pin-ended strut (Cambridge).
