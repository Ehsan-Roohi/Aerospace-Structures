# Dewoitine D.520 S — Descriptive and Operating Manual (engine 12 Y 45)

English translation of the scanned *Notice descriptive et d'utilisation de l'avion de chasse D.520 S, moteur 12 Y 45* (Archives départementales de la Haute-Garonne stamp on every sheet). 168 scanned pages: 61 typewritten pages and 107 pages of plates and fold-out cards. Every page was transcribed from the scan and translated; numbers are kept exactly as written, illegible items are marked [illegible], handwritten additions are marked as such.

*PDF page 4 · printed page 3*

> **What the photograph shows:** Front three-quarter view from the aircraft's STARBOARD (right) side: the nose points to the right of the picture and toward the camera, the camera being roughly 40–50° off the nose axis (estimate). The starboard wing dominates the foreground, reaching almost to the left edge of the print, and its upper surface is seen at a shallow angle with a large roundel near the tip; the port wing shows only as a short tapering stub beyond the nose on the right. Camera height is just above the near wingtip (tree-line horizon passes just above the tip and across the upper fuselage side; visual estimate ~1.4–1.6 m), horizon about 60% down the frame; perspective is noticeable (near wing large, normal to slightly wide focal impression, close range ~10–12 m, estimate). The three-blade propeller has one blade nearly vertical (12 o'clock), one at about 4 o'clock pointing down-right and the third hidden behind the nose (about 8 o'clock); the spinner is dark. The starboard cowling shows three rectangular exhaust apertures in a row high on the side and two horizontal slots lower down behind the spinner. Gear down: the near (starboard) wheel wears a white half-disc outer cover, the far (port) wheel shows its hub; the dark ventral radiator fairing is visible between the legs. The windscreen is followed by the arched front bow of the sliding hood standing well aft (canopy apparently open); a mast rises behind the cockpit and a short post stands ahead of the windscreen. In this print the fuselage and fin look uniformly pale/silvery (strong low sunlight from the right, long shadows to the right) while the wing upper surface reads dark; grass airfield, tree-lined horizon, partly cloudy sky.

## A- MAIN UNDERCARRIAGE - DESCRIPTION

The main undercarriage (Pl. No. 30) retracts into the wing, each half-undercarriage folding laterally inwards.

It is made up of two independent half-undercarriages, each attached to one of the wings, and comprising: a leg (C), a folding (breaking) strut (E) with return bungee, and a retraction jack (D).

A pressure-oil supply circuit ensures simultaneous operation of the two jacks (D) (Pl. No. 23).

The leg (C) turns on a fixed pin (B), screwed into the front nose-piece (A) and seated (embedded) in the spar (H).

The strut (E) turns on a pin integral with the leg (C) and on a pin (F) assembled with the spar (H). In the gear-down position it is braced (locked over-centre) by the return bungee (G), which is attached to the strut and to the spar (H).

The "OLAER" leg can work in compression, bending and torsion. At its lower end it carries the wheel stub axle (J), dog-engaged and clamped on the sliding tube (I).

The shock absorber proper is inside the leg (C). Its inflation valve, which is accessible, emerges on the outside of the leg body.

## B- RETRACTION OF THE MAIN UNDERCARRIAGE

The undercarriage retracts into the wing by means of the double-acting jacks (D), into which pressure oil is sent, supplied by a self-regulating micro-pump driven by the engine.

Retraction of the undercarriage takes place as follows: by operating the control lever of the undercarriage selector valve, the jack (D) is fed through the union located on the head of the jack (the side opposite the leg). The jack, locked when the undercarriage is down, unlocks and extends.

Acted on by the jack, the leg (C) turns about (B); the strut (E) pivots on the pin (F), breaks, and raises the leg.

The undercarriage is lowered by sending oil to the other face of the piston (inlet on the leg (C) side). The jack shortens and performs the reverse of the preceding movements. The strut unfolds and braces itself by means of the return bungee (G). The jack locks hydraulically in the gear-down position.

## F - POWERPLANT -

The aircraft is fitted with a HISPANO-SUIZA 12 Y 45 engine-cannon (moteur-canon) engine. It has a three-bladed RATIER propeller, variable in pitch in flight, with electric control. It is separated from the cockpit by a rigorously sealed fireproof bulkhead.

### Controls - (Pl.33)

The controls, of the TELEFLEX type, are located in the cockpit on the left-hand side.

They comprise the throttle and ignition (contact) controls.

### Starter - (Pl.34)

The starter is a VIET 250 carburetted-air starter. It works by injecting a mixture of air and petrol into the cylinders.

Ignition of this mixture is produced by the starting (booster) coil. Apart from the engine itself, the installation comprises:

A compressed-air reservoir, a hand pump, a sprayer (atomiser) and an injection pump, a relief valve, a compressor on the engine, and a distributor mounted in line with (at the end of) the camshaft.

### Coolant circulation (Pl.35)

The water leaving the cylinder heads flows down to the engine pump; two de-aerators (degassers) in by-pass collect the steam and condense it in a horseshoe-shaped header tank on the reduction gear.

This condensed water falls back into the circuit. A safety valve prevents excess pressure in the header tank. From the pump the water is delivered to the radiator, from which it comes out to rise back to the cylinder heads.

The radiator, attached under the fuselage, is faired so as to increase its efficiency. At the rear, a flap controlled by the pilot regulates the airflow.

### Oil circulation - (Pl. 36)

The oil, starting from the tank, passes through a G.M. heater-filter and then goes to the crankcase. It then leaves the crankcase to go to the radiator and returns to the tank. A shunt (by-pass) on the radiator lets the surplus oil through. A G.M. regulator is installed in the oil tank.

The (oil) radiator is attached inside a fairing under the engine. The air is captured and ducted through a scoop (manche).

## Starting operation (with vernier-setting table)
*PDF page 36 · printed page 28*

### Starting operation

With the starter-and-services air reservoir under pressure, these operations comprise, for a first start: injection of petrol into the inlet manifolds, in two stages: suction and injection.

### Suction

Set the key to "Aspirer" (Suck); slowly pull the injection-pump piston upwards.

### Injection

Set the key to "Injecter" (Inject); press slowly and fully down on the injection-pump piston.

### Starting

Set the key to "Démarreur" (Starter). Open the "Réservoir démarreur" (Starter reservoir) needle valve, check the position of the mixture-adjustment knob, switch on the ignition, set the throttle beforehand, open the starting needle valve.

The compressed air from the bottle passes into the sprayer, mixes with the petrol and, passing through the magneto distributor, turns the engine over. At the same time a by-pass tapping on the carburetted air operates the starting contactor, which sends current into the starting (booster) coil and from there into the running magnetos.

After starting, close the starting needle valve and the shut-off needle valve, and empty the injection pump by pressing on the piston with the key on "Injecter". ## Causes of faulty operation (engine starting); 3. Ground run-up tests
*PDF page 37 · printed page 29*

**Causes of faulty operation.-**

a) **The engine turns over but does not start; there is no firing at all.**

Check the ignition installation. Turn the starting magneto and make sure that current reaches the spark-plug terminals. If it does, check whether the spark jumps across the electrodes. If there are no sparks, check the mounting and operation of the starting magneto, the insulation of the leads and the ignition distributor.

If the ignition is good: check the starter, the atomiser (sprayer) and the injection pump; check the position of the adjusting vernier.

Make sure the injection pump is primed (filled); if it is properly filled, the pump piston must not go down by more than one centimetre when pressed. Unscrew the throttling (restrictor) nut and examine the jet, which must be full, continuous and sufficiently gasified that no liquid falls back; the upper part of the jet must end by rising in the form of vapour. Operate the adjusting vernier to make sure it is effective.

b) The engine does not turn over: with the reservoir under pressure and the distributor correctly adjusted, unscrew the lubricating plug of the distributor and inject a syringe-full of castor oil into it, or else unscrew the atomiser, i.e. the mixture outlet nut, introduce castor oil into the pipe, quickly screw the nut back on and immediately attempt a fresh start.

c) The engine fires a few times and then stops; these firings are those of the carburetted mixture. The cause of the faulty operation must be sought elsewhere than in the starter:

1/ Fuel is not reaching the carburettors,

2/ The ignition from the running magnetos is defective.

3/ There has not been too much fuel injection (black smoke at the exhaust). [sic - see notes]

4/ Insufficient fuel injection.
