Recent cavitation methods

Selected work from 2021–2025 gives concrete directions beyond the original 1993 panel formulation: smoother cavity geometry, adjoint optimization, three-dimensional cavity models and viscous coupling. Each addresses a particular physical problem and needs its own matched verification.

Implemented in this lab: potential-flow BEM with separate convergence and admission checks, including a pressure-target disk solver under a declared symmetric artificial-image closure. The 2D hydrofoil admission record separately checks the pressure on surfaces still labelled wetted.

Proposed research: the paper-specific algorithms below have not been established as implemented by this page. A pressure-controlled disk/image solution does not supply the detachment and natural closure of a smooth hydrofoil.

Belibassakis (2021), JMSE 9(9), 976.Selected publisher full-text sections reviewed, including the objective and boundary conditions. An inverse source-vorticity BEM adjusts B-spline cavity controls and cavitation number using continuous-adjoint sensitivities. The reported NACA 16-series comparisons prescribe cavity length. A direct candidate for smoother shape updates and efficient gradients. It remains a given-length inverse problem; a pressure-input solver also needs length selection and an admitted detachment/closure model.

Pasandidehfard (2024), Engineering Analysis with Boundary Elements 168, 105912.Publisher and DOI metadata verified. Full method and primary abstract were not reviewed. The verified title identifies a three-dimensional iterative BEM for cylindrical bodies at incidence. No unseen update equation, closure prescription or accuracy result is inferred here. Relevant to the requested cylinder geometry and non-axisymmetric flow. Obtain and verify the full formulation before porting it; the current axisymmetric ring model does not cover a cylinder at nonzero incidence.
A panel method for the hydrodynamic analysis of a cavitating propeller with sheet and developed tip vortex cavitationS. Kim & S. A. Kinnas (2023), Physics of Fluids 35(12), 127118.Publisher-deposited abstract and DOI metadata reviewed; full algorithm not reviewed. A potential-flow panel model includes sheet and developed tip-vortex cavities together on a propeller. The reported study is steady. A route toward coupled three-dimensional cavity types. It supplies neither an implemented tip-vortex model in this lab nor a demonstrated repair of the present hydrofoil detachment problem.
Propeller performance prediction via 3D coupling of BEM with boundary layer solverT. S. Wu & S. A. Kinnas (2025), Ocean Engineering 321, 120396.Publisher abstract and method summary reviewed; full coupling recipe not reviewed. A three-dimensional potential BEM interacts with the primarily two-dimensional XFOIL boundary-layer solver to include viscous effects on propeller pressures and performance. A concrete viscous–inviscid coupling direction. Transfer to a cavitating hydrofoil requires a separate treatment of separation, cavity boundaries and closure, with new verification.
A viscous vorticity method for predicting 2-D turbulent cavitating flowsR. You & S. A. Kinnas (2024), Ocean Engineering 309, 118521.Publisher abstract and introductory method excerpts reviewed. VISVE combines viscous vorticity transport, turbulent flow and mixture/cavitation modelling using a finite-volume method. An explicit scheme reconstructs pressure. This is FVM, not BEM. It is a different governing-model route for viscous cavitation. Its mixture and compressibility assumptions must be checked for any intended shedding or shock comparison.
Cavity Detachment from a Wedge with Rounded Edges and the Surface Tension EffectY. N. Savchenko, G. Y. Savchenko & Y. A. Semenov (2021), JMSE 9(11), 1253.Selected publisher full-text sections reviewed, especially §2.3–2.4. A hodograph/integral-equation formulation uses the Brillouin–Villat condition: the free-surface curvature at detachment is finite and matches the body curvature. Surface tension enters the dynamic condition. A useful rounded-edge detachment benchmark, rather than a BEM implementation. Match edge radius and Weber number before transferring the condition; finite capillarity changes the boundary-value problem.
Partial cavity shedding on a hydrofoil resulting from re-entrant flow and bubbly shock wavesA. Bhatt, H. Ganesh & S. L. Ceccio (2023), Journal of Fluid Mechanics 957, A28.Publisher abstract and selected experimental text reviewed. High-speed imaging and time-resolved X-ray measurements on NACA0015 identify re-entrant-flow and bubbly-shock routes to cavity pinch-off and shedding. Experiments identify physics that a shedding model must reproduce. A steady attached-cavity solve or an isolated-bubble calculation does not establish those mechanisms.

Proposed implementation sequence

The following is a research plan for this lab, not a claim that the cited papers use every added constraint.

  1. Reproduce a matched inverse benchmark first. Use the 2021 hydrofoil geometry, incidence, prescribed cavity length and transition/closure assumptions. Compare the complete cavity gap and signed pressure profiles, then perform mesh and independent off-node field checks.
  2. Test smooth geometry and its derivatives. Introduce cavity controls such as B-splines and minimize the cavity-pressure defect. Verify gradient components against finite differences before using an adjoint. Retain nonintersection and the lab's wetted-pressure condition Cp + σ ≥ 0; a small objective cannot replace these admission checks.
  3. Make the accepted branch pressure-controlled. Derive σ from pressure, speed and temperature-dependent fluid properties. Solve for cavity length and the detachment position under an explicit closure/selection model. Require an actual bracket or admitted continuation; reject unsupported targets. Use the rounded-wedge detachment problem as a separate, matched test before generalizing to a smooth hydrofoil.
  4. Add viscosity or full 3D geometry as separate stages. Verify a boundary-layer coupling on matched wetted cases before adding cavitation. Review the complete cylindrical-body and propeller algorithms before a true 3D implementation. Preserve the distinction between a dry-cylinder containment check and wetted flow around the cylinder.
  5. Choose an unsteady model for cloud shedding. Specify the re-entrant, mass-transfer and mixture-compressibility physics required by the experimental regime, then compare time-resolved quantities. This is a separate validation target from steady sheet geometry.
