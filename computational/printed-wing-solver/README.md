A transparent structural model of the **as-printed** MIE 446 semi-wing: the PLA shell, solid nose and trailing-edge strip, printed rod sleeves, carbon rods, ribs and module seams generated by the course CAD code (`mie446_wing` v1.1.1).

## What it computes

| Step | Method | Independent check |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-section at any span station | Same NACA equations and cavity rule as the CAD code | Printed mass 241.6 g vs 240.8 g from CadQuery; cut-section areas within 1.5 % |
| Area, centroid, second moments | Exact polygon integration, modulus-weighted; slip-fit rods add only their own EI | Closed-form rectangle-with-hole test |
| Torsion constant, shear centre, shear stress | 2-D finite-element warping analysis (`sectionproperties`) | Bredt–Batho thin-wall J (within 4–10 %); thin tube closed form; shear-resultant and sign-convention test |
| Loads | Schrenk / elliptic / planform / uniform / tip lift × load factor, inertia relief; or a tip force | Root reactions equal the applied resultants to machine precision |
| Deflection and twist | Direct integration of M/EI and T/GJ, dry seams as rotational springs | 1-D Hermite beam FEM (agreement < 0.1 %); PL³/3EI and spring closed forms |
| Natural frequencies | 1-D FEM with consistent mass, lumped rib, cap and sensor masses | Uniform-cantilever frequency |
| Screening checks | PLA tension/compression/shear, skin buckling (flat plate vs NASA SP-8007 knocked-down cylinder), rod bending, rib bearing and dowel shear at dry seams, epoxied-seam joint, deflection, twist | Hand checks in the notebook |

Units are mm, N, MPa and g.

## Baseline findings (class baseline, PLA upright card, hobby-grade rods, dry seams)

* Two slip-fit 4 mm rods supply only about 2 % of the root bending stiffness; the PLA skin carries the bending.
* Dry module seams roughly halve the tip stiffness (0.38 versus 0.85 N/mm with epoxied seams), and rod clearance can add a dead band of about 3 mm at the tip.
* The legacy Python/reference result selects the larger of flat-panel and equivalent-cylinder buckling capacities. Its U ≈ 1.4 for a 10 N tip force is an idealization comparison, **not a validated wing allowable**. Wing Lab now excludes this combined result from student governing/pass summaries and reports both comparisons separately; buckling remains unresolved. The same load brings the hobby-grade rods at the root-side seam close to their assumed bending strength (U ≈ 0.9).

Material cards are deliberately labelled placeholders; replace them with coupon data.

```python
import printed_wing_solver as pws

geom = pws.WingGeometry(naca="2412", semi_span_mm=450, root_chord_mm=160, tip_chord_mm=100)
res = pws.solve(geom, pws.PLA_UPRIGHT, pws.ROD_HOBBY,
                pws.Assembly(rods_bonded=False, seams_bonded=False),
                pws.LoadCase(kind="tip", tip_force_N=10.0, factor_of_safety=1.0))
print(res.summary())
for row in res.check_table():
    print(row["check"], row["utilization"], row["status"])
```

## Wing Lab: the same solver as a 3-D browser app

### Progressive learning pathway

The landing screen now starts with **cantilever → fixed–fixed → simply supported beams**, then **continuous wing skin only → rods and sleeves → ribs → the actual modular course wing**. The introductory wing comparisons share a 2 N tip force; the course preset restores the flight-like baseline. The skin-only example is one continuous shell, not disconnected printed modules with their rods removed.

The independent Euler–Bernoulli beam engine exposes support reactions, shear, bending moment, deflection, top/bottom normal stress, rectangular-section maximum shear stress, three bending modes, a position probe and a mesh-refinement comparison. Point forces and piecewise linear distributed loads can be superposed. Uniform and triangular presets hold total force constant to teach why its location still changes bending. Support changes preserve loads; use **Centre point · 2 N** for a clear comparison between support conditions. A study record includes inputs, results and the student's explanation.

In the wing workspace, **Build a wing load** supports point, rectangular, triangular and trapezoidal loads with span and chord location. Custom loads use upward-positive forces and do not silently add flight inertia relief. **Skin · rods · ribs · modes** explains load factor, governing equations and each component's role. Skin and rod stress displays are distinct; rib results are limited to the existing joint-bearing screen, not a fabricated full rib stress contour.

### Compare assumptions, not just answers

* **Local skin:** a one-way strip and a two-way Navier plate appear side by side, with support sketches, equations, centre stresses, displacement/thickness warnings and a series-convergence comparison. The plate assumes all four edges simply supported and isotropic small-deflection behaviour. Its default width is a skin arc length, not proof those supports exist. Neither is an actual local shell solution. Halving bay length divides strip displacement by 16 only under the strip assumptions; the plate response depends on aspect ratio. Values outside the classroom δ/t screen are marked as linear extrapolations, not predictions. These local displacements are not added to global wing bending.
* **Torsion:** plot the local shear-centre path, then test 0.25c, 0.30c and the computed zero-tip-twist load line. The tapered model integrates changing lever arms and joint spring rotations. Zero tip twist need not mean zero torque or zero rotation everywhere.
* **Ribs:** a controlled comparison removes editable interior ribs while retaining interface ribs and other inputs. It reports changes in mass, longest bay, root EI, tip displacement and frequency. Unchanged EI reflects the reduced model, not proof ribs have no structural role.
* **Buckling:** flat-panel and equivalent-cylinder U values are comparisons with distinct assumptions. The engine retains the legacy combined screen for reference reproducibility; student summaries and challenge feedback exclude it and explicitly state that stability is unresolved. A low other-check U does not establish wing safety.
* **Beam supports:** if all nonzero point loads lie on restrained ends, an explicit warning explains the zero response and offers a button to move them to midspan. Loads are never moved silently. Beam charts include units, ticks and a pointer/touch position readout.
* **Optional observations:** fit force–displacement pairs and compare stiffness and first bending frequency under matching conditions. No laboratory is required. The example data are explicitly synthetic, and nothing is uploaded. Page refresh loses entered data.

## Limits

Linear elastic, small deflection, plane sections between ribs, one spanwise modulus for printed PLA, uncoupled bending and torsion dynamics, no post-buckling, stress concentrations, shear lag or creep. The dry-seam spring (rods bending over an effective free length) and the clearance dead band are estimates with uncertain inputs. This is a teaching analysis: it does not authorise loading, flying or testing a student wing.
