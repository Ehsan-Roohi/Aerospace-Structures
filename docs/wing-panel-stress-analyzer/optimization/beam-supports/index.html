Support Placement and Worst-Case Load Position

beam-supports 0.1.1
Scope. You have solved a beam with given supports. This page treats the support positions as design variables: it finds the placement that minimizes the peak bending moment or the peak deflection (Part A) and then asks where a moving load does the most harm, using influence lines (Part B). Model: Euler-Bernoulli beam, linear elastic, small deflection, uniform EI (flexural rigidity, E from the material card times I from the section), 2-node Hermite cubic finite elements with at least 120 elements, rigid supports (pin: v = 0; clamp: v = 0 and slope = 0). Sign convention: x to the right, y up, loads and reactions positive upward, bending moment positive when sagging (tension at the bottom), shear V = dM/dx, deflection positive upward. Every recommended layout is re-solved from scratch with the exact solver before it is shown. The stress and deflection utilizations use representative educational material cards: nothing here is a certified strength, stability or fatigue statement, and this page is independent of the Wing AeroStruct solver.

How it works

Model and sign convention

The beam is solved with 2-node Hermite cubic Euler-Bernoulli finite elements (FE). Nodes are placed at the beam ends, at every support, at every load point and UDL end and at the section x_s, and each segment is refined so that at least 120 elements span the beam. Loads are applied through consistent (work-equivalent) load vectors, so the nodal displacements and rotations of this element are exact for the Euler-Bernoulli beam. The reactions follow from the exact nodal solution, and the shear and moment diagrams are recovered by statics from the loads and reactions, which makes them exact as well. The deflection between nodes is the Hermite cubic of the element.

A layout with fewer than two effective constraints (two pins at different positions or one clamp) is a mechanism and is reported as an error. Supports closer than a quarter of an element length are merged with a warning.

Convention: x to the right, y up. Loads, reactions and deflections are positive upward, the bending moment is positive when sagging (tension at the bottom), the shear is the sum of the upward forces to the left of the section, so V = dM/dx. The bending stress is |M| c / I and its utilization is |sigma| FS / sigma_allow; the deflection utilization is |v| / (L / ratio). A layout is called feasible when both utilizations are at most 1.

Part A: how the optimum is found and checked

A1, symmetric overhang. The overhang a is swept over 400 values from 0 to 0.49 L. The best grid point of each curve (peak |M| and peak |v|) is refined by golden-section search inside its neighbouring interval, then the refined layout is re-solved from scratch and compared with the optimizer's value before it is shown ("propose, then verify").

A2, two free supports. The peak objective is evaluated on a 60 by 60 grid of (a1, a2); the half with a1 at or beyond a2 is infeasible and hatched. The grid runs in small time slices so the page stays responsive. The best cell starts a Nelder-Mead simplex search whose trial points are projected onto 0 <= a1, a1 + 0.02 L <= a2 <= L. The result is verified by a fresh solve.

A3, three supports. Pins at both ends plus one interior pin at s, swept over 400 values from 0.02 L to 0.98 L, refined and verified in the same way. At s = L/2 under a UDL the page checks the two-span textbook values.

The governing condition at a minimax optimum is a tie: for the moment objective the largest hogging and the largest sagging moments become equal; for the deflection objective the largest upward and downward deflections (or two downward peaks) become equal. The page reports the ratio of the two peaks so you can see which condition is active.

Part B: influence lines and the worst-case load position

An influence line (IL) for a quantity at a fixed section x_s gives that quantity as a function of the position x_p of a single moving load. The page moves the load P over 200 positions on the chosen support layout, with all other loads removed (the response to the other loads can be superposed afterwards because the model is linear), and computes M(x_s), V(x_s), v(x_s) and the reaction at one support. The position with the largest |value| is refined by golden-section search and confirmed by a fresh solve.

At a section that coincides with the load or with a support the shear jumps; the plotted value is the mean of the left and right limits, and the worst case is approached from one side. By the Maxwell-Betti reciprocal theorem the deflection IL at x_s equals the deflected shape under a unit load placed at x_s (Mueller-Breslau principle).

Classical results used as checks
  • Simply supported beam, full-span UDL: midspan moment w L^2 / 8, midspan deflection 5 w L^4 / (384 EI).
  • Two symmetric supports under a full-span UDL: the overhang a = (sqrt(2) - 1) / 2 L = 0.2071 L makes the hogging and sagging peaks equal, at 0.0214 w L^2 instead of 0.125 w L^2 with the supports at the ends, a reduction of about 83 percent. The deflection optimum is different; the sweep locates it (about 0.223 L for the UDL alone).
  • Two equal spans (pins at 0, L/2, L) under a UDL: hogging moment over the middle support w L^2 / 32, end reactions 3 w L / 16, middle reaction 10 w L / 16 = 5 w L / 8, span sagging peak 9 w L^2 / 512.
  • Moment influence line at midspan of a simply supported beam: a triangle with peak L / 4 per unit load.
Limitations
  • Euler-Bernoulli kinematics only: no shear deformation, no large deflections, uniform EI, rigid supports without settlement, loads in one plane.
  • The stress and deflection utilizations are screening indicators with educational material cards, not design checks; there is no lateral-torsional buckling, local buckling, bearing, fatigue or dynamic amplification check.
  • The moving load of Part B is a single concentrated load; distributed moving loads and load trains are not covered.
  • Self-weight is included only if you put it into w.
