Truss Redundancy, Load Cases and Reliability

truss-failsafe 0.1.1
Scope. A design that passes one load case with a factor of safety FS = 1.5 can still fail when a bracing wire breaks, when the load case changes, or when the properties of printed or natural parts scatter. This page quantifies all three on your truss with a 2D pin-jointed linear elastic truss model (direct stiffness method): stress utilization U = |N| FS / (A sigma_allow), an Euler buckling screen for solid square members (P_cr = pi^2 E I / (K L)^2, I = A^2 / 12, assumed effective length factor K), and tension-only bracing wires. Member forces are axial only, joints are frictionless pins, displacements are small, and there is no pretension, no dynamic load transfer at the instant of failure, no fatigue, and no certified strength data. The Monte Carlo part is an educational reliability demonstration with assumed lognormal distributions, not a certification. Every design shown is re-solved from scratch with the exact solver before it is displayed. Independent of the Wing AeroStruct solver.

How it works

Model and utilization
Direct stiffness method for a 2D pin-jointed truss: each bar contributes E A / L along its axis, supports remove degrees of freedom (DOF), and the free system is solved by Gaussian elimination with pivoting. A pivot that vanishes relative to the diagonal, or a failed residual check, means a mechanism: the page reports "unstable structure" instead of numbers. Member force N = (E A / L) times the elongation along the axis, tension positive, stress N / A. Utilization: tension U = |N| FS / (A sigma_allow); compression U = max(stress utilization, |N| FS / P_cr) with P_cr = pi^2 E I / (K L)^2 and I = A^2 / 12 for solid square members (a screening indicator with an assumed effective length factor, not a stability analysis); groups marked "none" use the stress check only. Mass = sum of A L rho / 1000 in grams. A tension-only wire that the linear model puts in compression is flagged slack: the linear model is then not valid for that wire. With the "tension-only" wire model such wires are dropped and the structure is re-solved until no wire is in compression; compressions below 0.1 percent of the largest member force count as zero so unloaded bays keep their wires.
Part A: fail-safe map
For the primary case, each member is deleted in turn (area set to zero) and the damaged truss is re-solved. The table records the maximum utilization of the remaining members, which member now governs, the displacement of the reference node (tip or midspan) relative to the intact truss, and whether the damaged truss is a mechanism ("collapse"). The damage tolerance index is the fraction of single-member removals after which every remaining utilization stays at or below 1. The count m + r - 2j (members, reaction components, joints) is the degree of static indeterminacy: a value of 0 means every member is needed for stability, so every removal is a mechanism; a positive value is necessary but not sufficient for redundancy, and the solve decides.
Part B: load-case envelope and fully stressed design
All checked cases are solved; the envelope is the elementwise maximum utilization over the cases and the governing case per member is the one that produces it. The fully stressed design (FSD) iteration A_i <- A_i U_i (stress) and A_i <- A_i sqrt(U_buckle,i) (buckling, because P_cr grows with A^2) takes the larger factor, clamps to A_min = 0.2 A0 and runs up to 40 iterations. Because a uniform scale-up of all areas leaves the member forces of a linear truss unchanged, a final uniform scale by max(U_stress, sqrt(U_buckle)) makes the governing member exactly fully stressed if the iteration has not converged. For a statically determinate truss the forces do not depend on the areas, so FSD converges in one step and (with stress constraints only) is the minimum mass design. "Size for one case, check under all" sizes for the primary case, then evaluates the result under every other checked case; sizing for the envelope uses the maximum utilization over all cases in every iteration, and the mass difference is the price of covering every case.
Part C: Monte Carlo reliability
Each sample draws, per group, a modulus E and an allowable stress sigma_allow from lognormal distributions with the given coefficient of variation (CoV = standard deviation / mean) and the mean preserved, and one load multiplier with the load CoV; the pseudo-random numbers come from a seeded mulberry32 generator, so a given seed always reproduces the same result. The truss is solved for every sample and the maximum design utilization U (FS included) over all members is recorded. Two events are counted: the design criterion is violated when max U > 1, and the limit state is exceeded when max U > FS, which means the actual stress exceeds the actual strength or the actual load exceeds the buckling load. P_f is the fraction of samples in the event with a 95 percent Wilson score interval; the reliability index is beta = -Phi^-1(P_f) with Phi^-1 from Acklam's rational approximation and Phi from an erf approximation. The sensitivity table re-runs the study with each scatter source switched off in turn, with the same random numbers, and the run with all scatter off must reproduce the deterministic maximum utilization exactly. Classical check: for lognormal load S and resistance R with central safety factor theta = mean R / mean S, beta = ln(theta sqrt((1 + V_S^2) / (1 + V_R^2))) / sqrt(ln((1 + V_R^2)(1 + V_S^2))); applied per member it gives the first-order series-system bounds max_i P_i <= P_f <= sum_i P_i, shown beside the Monte Carlo estimate. The members of one group share one sampled E and one sampled strength (a batch of material), so their failures are strongly correlated and P_f tends to sit near the lower bound.
Classical references and limitations
Direct stiffness truss analysis: any structural analysis text (for example Hibbeler, Structural Analysis). Fully stressed design and its optimality for determinate trusses under stress constraints: Haftka and Gurdal, Elements of Structural Optimization. Lognormal reliability index and series-system bounds: Melchers and Beck, Structural Reliability Analysis and Prediction; Nowak and Collins, Reliability of Structures. Limitations: pin-jointed bars only (real spars are continuous and carry bending), no wire pretension (the Wright Flyer wires were rigged taut, which keeps both wires of a crossed pair active), removal is static (no dynamic overshoot when a member snaps), the Euler screen ignores end fixity and imperfections, material cards are representative educational values, the scatter model (lognormal, independent between groups, one batch per group) is an assumption. Nothing here is a certified analysis.
