Method, scope & evidence

This is an independent experimental module. It does not change the published Wing Lab or provide a three-dimensional cavitating-wing solution.

The potential satisfies Laplace's equation in the liquid. Wetted panels impose no penetration. Cavity panels impose total potential integrated from the prescribed tangential speed. A mixed source/doublet boundary-element system solves wetted potential, cavity normal derivative and cavity speed. The actual cavity contour is updated until the discrete normal velocity and shape correction converge.

On the constant-pressure portion: qc/U = √(1 + σ), Cp = −σ. The final λ fraction uses an explicit algebraic pressure-recovery model. A, λ and ν affect the answer; these example settings are not fitted to experimental data. No re-entrant jet has been implemented.

Smooth contact adds one unknown, the recovery attenuation A, and one equation: the physical cavity gap has zero extrapolated slope at its prescribed endpoint. A bracketed search solves that equation around the initial A. Inner residual tolerances are tightened to at most 1e-7 (normal) and 1e-8 (shape). The accepted branch must also have a nonnegative cavity gap; an equation-converged intersecting branch is rejected. The initial fixed-A shape and all rejected trials remain in the result data.

This is an explicit additional closure-selection assumption, not a resolved re-entrant jet and not a claim to reproduce the original PCPAN implementation. Some combinations of section, angle, detachment and cavity length have no admitted smooth-contact branch. Fixed A remains available for historical comparisons. Case files without a closure/TE setting preserve fixed-A/Morino physics. No stored result is treated as a new calculation.

The new case uses an independent wake potential jump and a velocity Kutta equation: opposite-oriented tangential speeds at the first and last trailing-edge collocation points sum to zero. Both use five-point derivatives of total potential. The legacy Morino potential-jump condition remains selectable. This change improves mesh consistency; it does not establish physical cavity validation.

For fixed length and detachment, σ is an output. The corresponding absolute pressure is p∞ = pv + σρU²/2. It is not an independently prescribed input in this version.

The combined-contour lift and pitching moment are exploratory. Its near-zero pressure drag is not a prediction of real cavitation drag. Viscosity, nuclei, free-surface effects, unsteady shedding, collapse, erosion and structural coupling are outside this prototype.

The residuals are internal numerical checks, not errors against measurements. A converged branch can still be physically inappropriate. An intersecting cavity or an unconverged iteration is rejected without clipping Cp or cavity height.

Evidence: original analytic regressions, endpoint mesh check, velocity-Kutta analytic check, independent velocity comparison. These include analytic noncavitating cylinder/Joukowski benchmarks and cavity mesh/iteration tests. The identical-contour independent check is not a complete free-boundary validation. The independent cavity benchmark has NOT PASSED: a converged alternative branch intersects the foil and is rejected. Experimental validation and re-entrant-jet comparisons remain outstanding.

Sources: Kinnas & Fine (1993); Kinnas, chapter 9; Krishnaswamy, chapter 3.

Residuals & interpretation

The pressure derivative is taken separately within wet and cavity regions. The detachment/closure junctions and the recovery zone are discretization-sensitive; no experimental pressure accuracy is claimed.
