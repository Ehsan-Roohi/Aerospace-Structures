Beam Section Design-Space Map

beam-section-map 0.1.1
Scope. This page places the beam cross-section you sized by hand on a map of the whole design space and shows which constraint governs, how far your design is from the minimum-mass design, and the mass against deflection trade-off. The model is an Euler-Bernoulli beam (linear elastic, small deflection, constant section, one static load) evaluated with closed-form moment and deflection expressions. Only three checks are made: bending stress against the allowable stress divided by the factor of safety (FS), maximum deflection against L/k, and a minimum printable wall or dimension t_min. It does not check shear, lateral-torsional or local buckling, fatigue or any certified strength, and it is independent of the Wing AeroStruct solver. Every optimum shown here is re-solved from scratch with the exact closed forms before it is displayed; nothing on this page is a certified strength or safety statement.

How it works

Model and equations

Positions are measured from x = 0, the clamp of a cantilever or the left support of a simply supported beam. Loads are entered as magnitudes: in this linear model a load acting up or down gives the same utilizations, so the sign convention (y up, loads positive upward, sagging moment positive) does not change any number on this page. The maximum bending moment M_max and the maximum deflection v_max come from the classical closed forms (a is the load position, b = L minus a the distance to the other support, w the load per unit length, E the Young modulus, I the second moment of area):

cantilever, point load at a: M_max = P a (at the clamp), v_tip = P a^2 (3L - a) / (6 E I)
cantilever, uniform load: M_max = w L^2 / 2, v_tip = w L^4 / (8 E I)
simply supported, point load: M_max = P a b / L, v_max = P b (L^2 - b^2)^(3/2) / (9 sqrt(3) L E I), b = distance to the nearer support
simply supported, uniform: M_max = w L^2 / 8, v_max = 5 w L^4 / (384 E I)

With the extreme-fibre distance c and the area A of the section: sigma_max = M_max c / I, and the three utilizations are

U_stress = sigma_max FS / sigma_allow, U_defl = v_max / (L / k), U_thick = t_min / (smallest wall or dimension)
mass (g) = A (mm^2) L (mm) rho (g/cm^3) / 1000

A design is feasible when every U is at most 1. A utilization between 0.9 and 1 is flagged as near the limit. The section families are a solid rectangle (b, h), a circular tube (outer diameter D, wall t) and a rectangular box (depth h, wall t, width b = beta h).

Algorithm: map, refinement, verification

Map. The two section parameters are sampled on an n by n grid over the ranges in the inputs and every cell is evaluated exactly with the closed forms above. The colour is the mass, the white lines are iso-mass contours, and the coloured lines are the three constraint boundaries U = 1 (red: stress, orange: deflection, grey: minimum thickness). Hatched cells violate at least one constraint. Cells where the geometry is impossible (for example a tube wall thicker than D/2) are drawn light grey.

Refinement. The lightest feasible grid cell is the starting point of a boundary-following pattern search. Because every utilization decreases monotonically when a section grows, for any trial value of the first parameter the smallest feasible second parameter is found exactly by bisection on max(U) = 1. A compass search with step halving then moves the first parameter until no step (down to one ten-millionth of the range) lowers the mass. All trial points are feasible by construction, so no penalty function is needed. The search is deterministic and always gives the same answer for the same inputs.

Verification. The refined design is then re-solved from scratch with the exact closed forms and all three constraints are re-checked. Only that verified result is displayed, with the badge "Checked by fresh model solve". If the re-check ever failed, the page would fall back to the best grid cell and say so. The "nearest lighter feasible design" is the feasible grid cell lighter than your design that minimizes the Euclidean distance in parameter space, with each parameter normalized by its map range; it is verified the same way.

Governing constraint. At the optimum the constraint with the utilization closest to 1 governs. The minimum thickness is often active at the same time, because for all three families the lightest section is the thinnest one the printer allows, made as deep (or as large in diameter) as the stress or deflection boundary requires. When the optimum sits on an edge of your map range the page says so; widening the range may then lower the mass further.

Pareto panel

The mass against deflection panel treats deflection as a second objective instead of a constraint. All grid cells that satisfy the stress and minimum-thickness constraints are candidates. A candidate is non-dominated (on the Pareto front) when no other candidate is both lighter and stiffer. The front is found by sorting the candidates by mass and keeping every point whose deflection is lower than all lighter points. Your design is "dominated by N designs" when N candidates are at least as light and at least as stiff, with one of the two strictly better. The orange dashed line is your deflection limit L/k: front points below it are feasible designs.

Material comparison and performance indices

For every material card the same grid, refinement and verification are run with that card's E, allowable stress and density, over the same map range, so the minimum masses are directly comparable (a card with no feasible design inside the range is reported as such). The two classical Ashby indices for a beam of fixed length are also listed: E^(1/2)/rho for a stiffness-limited beam and sigma_allow^(2/3)/rho for a strength-limited beam, each normalized to the best card.

The indices assume that the section can be scaled freely at a fixed shape, with no minimum thickness and no map limits. The exact minimum masses include t_min, the section family and the map range, so the two rankings can differ. Comparing the index ranking with the exact ranking is the point of the panel. The steel bracing-wire card is a tension-member card and is included only for completeness.

Limitations and references
  • No shear deformation, no shear stress check, no self-weight, a single static load case and a constant section along the span.
  • No lateral-torsional buckling check: the model rewards very deep, narrow rectangles. No local buckling check: the model rewards very thin walls. The slenderness ratio shown next to each design is a screening number only, not a pass or a fail.
  • The material cards are representative educational values, not certified allowables. Printed parts are anisotropic; use measured coupon data for real work.
  • The optimum is the lightest design inside the map range. If it sits on a range edge, the true unconstrained optimum of this model lies outside the map.
  • Closed forms: J. M. Gere and S. P. Timoshenko, Mechanics of Materials (deflection tables), and W. C. Young and R. G. Budynas, Roark's Formulas for Stress and Strain. Material indices: M. F. Ashby, Materials Selection in Mechanical Design, chapter on material indices for beams. Pattern search: R. Hooke and T. A. Jeeves, "Direct search" solution of numerical and statistical problems, J. ACM 8 (1961).
