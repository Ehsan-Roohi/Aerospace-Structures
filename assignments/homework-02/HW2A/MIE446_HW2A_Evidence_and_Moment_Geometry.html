Preparation · Read before calculating any moment

Coordinate axes and moment reference points

Notebook route: find 2D. Aircraft rotations, 2E. Center of gravity, then 7A-3b. A vertical vane is not automatically a pure-yaw device. Use Ctrl+F (Command+F on Mac) to find the section title. Read Figure 7A-3b-1 before the equations.

Lecture 1, Figure 7A-3b-1. Aircraft right means the pilot’s right looking forward. An axis drawn into the page is not absent.

Use a right-handed body-axis system: +x forward, +y right, +z down. Positive \(M_x\) tends to lower the right wing; positive \(M_y\) tends to raise the nose; positive \(M_z\) tends to turn the nose right. A moment tendency does not by itself predict the complete flight trajectory.

\[\mathbf M_{CG}=\mathbf r\times\mathbf F,\quad \mathbf r=(x,y,z).\] \[M_x=yF_z-zF_y,\quad M_y=zF_x-xF_z,\quad M_z=xF_y-yF_x.\]

The coordinates in \(\mathbf r\) run from the CG to the force application point. Use metres and newtons, giving N·m. A positive coordinate locates a point; it is not automatically the perpendicular lever arm of every force component.

Do not carry coordinate signs between different problems without checking. HW1’s airfoil coordinates had x from leading edge to trailing edge and y upward. In HW 2, Parts 2–4 use body axes and moments about the CG; Part 5 uses a local aerodynamic moment about a vane pivot. The historical paper reports its own aerodynamic-axis coefficients; retain those labels and signs in Part 1. A lowercase roll coefficient \(c_l\) is not the lift coefficient \(C_L\).

Practice example — different numbers from the assessed cases

A force \(\mathbf F=(0,0,3)\) N acts at \(\mathbf r=(0.20,0,0)\) m. Then \(M_x=0\), \(M_y=-(0.20)(3)=-0.60\) N·m, and \(M_z=0\). Sketch why a downward force ahead of the CG produces a nose-down tendency.

r_demo = [0.20, 0, 0];       % row vector, m
F_demo = [0, 0, 3];          % row vector, N
M_demo = cross(r_demo,F_demo); % returns [Mx My Mz]

cross(F_demo,r_demo) gives the opposite sign. Use the physical definition \(\mathbf r\times\mathbf F\), not whichever order produces the expected answer.

1A. Identify the hardware [4 points]

  1. Choose one historical photograph or the lecture’s labeled conceptual plan view. Record its source. Label the aft horizontal tail, aft vertical tail, wing-tip vane, and one control-linkage route. If a component is hidden in your photograph, say “not visible in this view” and add a separate labeled sketch.
  2. Write two or three sentences distinguishing tail presence, surface motion, and control purpose. Explain why the existence of a wing-tip rudder does not imply absence of an aft tail.

1B. Extract and interpret six pairs of coefficients [7 points]

  1. In Figure 11, locate the roll-coefficient axis and the two marker series: filled circles (●): spoileron, 90°; crosses (×): wing warping. In Figure 12, find the corresponding yaw-coefficient series. Do not use the open-square rudder markers (□) in this comparison.
  2. At \(\alpha=6^\circ,10^\circ,14^\circ\), read the marker center for each mechanism. Use the nearest grid ticks to estimate the value. Read the measured markers, not the dashed fitted/trend line. Keep the plotted sign. Report sensible precision: graph reading does not justify many decimals.
  3. Enter the six rows in the Excel Paper sheet, rows 7:12. Columns A/B identify angle and mechanism; enter roll coefficient in C, yaw coefficient in D, reading tolerance in E, and figure/marker notes in F. In E, write separate roll and yaw tolerances if their plot scales differ, for example “roll ±…; yaw ±…”.
  4. Use grid spacing and marker width to justify your tolerance. Compare one row with a teammate’s independent reading. If values differ by more than the declared reading tolerance, revisit the figure together and record the resolution. Tolerance describes your reading, not measurement scatter.
  5. Explain the observed difference between the two mechanisms’ roll–yaw coupling over these selected points. For each mechanism, do roll and yaw have the same sign or opposite signs, and how does Section II.C explain it? Cite the figure and the test configuration (model, tunnel, flow speed) from the opening of Section II. Do not claim a universal response at every angle of attack or every spoileron deflection.

Keep these coefficients nondimensional; compare signs and trends, not sizes. Record the paper’s labels and axis convention; no conversion to N·m is required. The paper does not state the reference area and lengths behind \(c_l\) and \(c_n\), and the two figures use very different scales, so a larger \(c_n\) does not by itself mean a larger yawing than rolling moment.

Choose one of MQ-9 or X-48B and one of X-36, X-47B, or MAGMA from the notebook. Make a two-row table with: aircraft; visible tail/canard/winglet arrangement; one documented control mechanism; physical quantity it changes; and structure receiving the reaction.
