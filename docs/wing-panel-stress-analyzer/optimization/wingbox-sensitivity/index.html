Wing Box Layout: Sensitivities, Active Constraints and Shadow Prices

wingbox-sensitivity 0.1.1
Scope. This page sizes a straight, untwisted, cantilevered half wing with its own simplified thin-walled wing box model: a single rectangular cell between 15 and 60 percent chord, spar caps and skins treated as lumped flanges in bending (modulus weighted, webs ignored in bending), Bredt-Batho shear flow and torsion constant, Euler-Bernoulli bending of a linear elastic cantilever, an elliptic lift distribution and a plate buckling screen with simply supported edges. It is written for this page alone and is independent of the Wing AeroStruct solver on this site: there is no panel method, no coupled aeroelasticity and no certified strength data (the material cards are educational values). Its purpose is to show what sensitivities, active constraints and Lagrange multipliers (shadow prices) mean for a wing layout. Every design presented here is re-solved from scratch with the same solver before it is shown; nothing here is a certified strength or safety statement.

How it works

Model and loads

The half wing is a cantilever clamped at the root with a linear chord taper from c_r to c_t. The spar box spans 15 to 60 percent chord, so its width is w(y) = 0.45 c(y) and its height is h(y) = 0.9 tau c(y). The half wing carries half of the aircraft load: L_half = n m g / 2 with g = 9.81 m/s^2, distributed elliptically, l(y) = l_0 sqrt(1 - (y/s)^2) with l_0 = 4 L_half / (pi s). Shear V(y), bending moment M(y) and torque T(y) are integrated numerically from the tip with the trapezoidal rule on 201 stations. The torque per unit span is the section pitching moment q c^2 c_m (q = 0.5 rho V^2 with rho = 1.225 kg/m^3; the airspeed is used only here) plus the lift offset: lift acts at 25 percent chord, the box shear centre is taken at its mid-width (37.5 percent chord), so lift acts 12.5 percent chord ahead of the shear centre. Nose-up torque is positive; lift and deflection are positive upward.

At each station the bending inertia uses the caps and skins as lumped flanges, I = 2 (2 A_cap)(h/2)^2 + 2 w t_s (h/2)^2, and the bending stiffness is modulus weighted, EI = E_cap I_cap + E_skin I_skin. The outer fibre strain is M (h/2) / EI, so the cap stress is E_cap times that strain and the skin stress is E_skin times it (when both moduli are equal this is the usual M (h/2) / I). Web shear flow is V / (2 h), torsion shear flow is Bredt's T / (2 A_m) with A_m = w h; the web shear stress is their sum over t_w and the skin shear stress is the torsion flow over t_s. Stress utilization U_stress is the largest of cap bending, skin bending, web shear (times sqrt 3) and skin shear (times sqrt 3), each multiplied by the factor of safety and divided by the material allowable. The buckling screen compares the largest upper skin compression (times FS) with sigma_cr = k pi^2 E_skin / (12 (1 - nu^2)) (t_s / b)^2, where the plate width is the rib pitch b = s / n_r. Tip deflection integrates M / EI twice from the root, tip twist integrates T / (G J) with G = E_skin / (2 (1 + nu)) and the Bredt-Batho J = 4 A_m^2 / (2 w / t_s + 2 h / t_w). U_thick = t_min / min(t_s, t_w, t_r). Mass sums skins (2 w t_s), webs (2 h t_w), caps (4 A_cap) along the span plus n_r ribs of area w h and thickness t_r at y = b, 2b, ..., s.

Sensitivities (sensitivity profile)
Each design variable is perturbed one at a time by plus and minus 10 percent (plus and minus one rib for n_r) and every response is re-solved. The bars show the percent change of mass, tip deflection, tip twist and the largest structural utilization (stress, buckling, deflection, twist). The table lists elasticities d ln R / d ln x from the same two solves: an elasticity of 0.5 means that a 1 percent change of the variable changes the response by about 0.5 percent. Signs tell you which way to move; magnitudes tell you which variables matter.
Minimum-mass optimization (sequential linear programming)
For each rib count in the search range the continuous variables x = (t_s, t_w, A_cap) are sized for minimum mass subject to U_stress, U_buckle, U_defl and U_twist at most 1 and t_s, t_w at least t_min. The method is sequential linear programming with move limits: at the current point the mass and the four utilizations are linearized with central finite differences in the relative variables u_i = (x_i - x_i,k) / x_i,k; a proximal term scaled so that the unconstrained step has the length of the move limit turns the step problem into a tiny strictly convex quadratic program, which is solved exactly by enumerating candidate active sets (this avoids the vertex-to-vertex zigzag of a plain linear program when the optimum lies on a curved constraint). The trial point is pulled back onto any violated constraint by Newton corrections along the constraint gradients, so every accepted iterate is feasible and the mass decreases monotonically. The move limit starts at 20 percent, is halved when a step is rejected or when consecutive steps reverse direction, and the loop stops after 60 iterations or when the move limit falls below 1e-4. Rib counts are handled by an outer loop; ribs add mass but shorten the buckling panel. The best design is re-solved from scratch before it is displayed.
Active constraints, Lagrange multipliers and shadow prices
A constraint is active when its utilization is within 0.02 of 1 (or a thickness sits at t_min). At a constrained minimum the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) condition says that the mass gradient is a non-negative combination of the gradients of the active constraints: grad m = sum over j of lambda_j (-grad g_j) with g_j = U_j - 1. The page estimates the lambda_j by non-negative least squares from finite-difference gradients at the verified optimum and reports the residual of that balance (small means the point is a true KKT point). Each lambda_j is a shadow price: the grams of mass that one unit of relaxation of the normalized constraint would save, to first order. The page translates it into physical units, for example grams per millimetre of deflection limit (lambda divided by the limit), and then checks the first-order prediction by actually re-optimizing with the limit relaxed by 1 percent. Inactive constraints have zero shadow price: relaxing them saves nothing.
Closed forms shown beside the numerical results
Largest rib pitch that passes the buckling screen for the current skin: b_max = t_s sqrt(k pi^2 E / (12 (1 - nu^2) sigma FS)), where sigma is the largest upper skin compression; the minimum rib count is ceil(s / b_max). Fully stressed spar caps: A_cap(y) proportional to M(y) (as specified for this page) or to M(y) / h(y) (the exact fully stressed law for a tapered depth), with a floor at a fraction of the root cap. Analytic checks used in the tests: elliptic load integrates to L_half; uniform load on a constant chord gives v_tip = w s^4 / (8 EI); Bredt J equals 4 A^2 / (perimeter / t) for a uniform wall; plate buckling gives 25.85 MPa for E = 70000 MPa, nu = 0.33, t = 1 mm, b = 100 mm, k = 4; the multiplier of minimize x + y subject to x y at least 1 is 1 at (1, 1).
Limitations
Linear elastic, small deflections, no coupling between bending and torsion, no aeroelastic feedback (the lift distribution does not change with twist), no rib or web buckling, no crippling of the caps, no joints, no fatigue, no damage tolerance. The plate buckling check is a screen: k = 4 is the long simply supported plate value, the plate width is taken as the rib pitch, and printed or laminated skins behave differently from the idealized isotropic plate. The material cards are representative values for teaching, not certified allowables. The thickness bound protects printability only; nothing here checks manufacturing.
