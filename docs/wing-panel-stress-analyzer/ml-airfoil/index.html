Scope before ranking

The paper specifies Re > 1 million, α from −4° to 12°, Mach from 0 to 0.9. ADAPT does not take Reynolds number as input. Its uncertainty does not cover low-Re model error. A 900 mm Aeroclub wing near Re 400,000 is outside this published domain.

No finite-wing induced drag, total drag, structural response or flight approval is provided here. Candidate geometries must be re-solved in Wing Lab and checked against appropriate low-Re data before design use.

Panel geometry and edge treatment

The sharp-edge solver uses a smooth closed-TE approximation for finite-gap sections; the change is disclosed here. ML keeps its original geometry. Endpoint Cp is not a convergence test: a finite-angle sharp edge tends toward stagnation at its exact point. Compare fixed x/c positions across meshes. Can this be used for a finite wing?

A 2D section cₗ is not full-wing C_L; no section result is relabelled as a wing result. Wing Lab already solves 3D panel pressures, lift, pitching moment and induced drag. A three-method wing comparison additionally needs matching 3D geometry, spanwise Cp stations and operating conditions, plus a separately validated wing ML model. Large Wing Model is a possible research extension; it is not installed or validated here. A transonic ONERA M6 CFD case cannot validate this incompressible Aeroclub wing.

Predictions use Mach 0.15, or 0.059 for the TR-613 context. Changing Re only checks scope; it does not re-run or alter the model. The reference point is a design probe, not a computed shear centre.

Reproducibility and limitations

Pressure forces and moments are integrated over both sampled surfaces. Force/moment covariance is propagated from the full joint Cp covariance; point uncertainties are not assumed independent. Moment uses nose-up positive about the quarter-chord before transfer to the chosen reference point. Bands are pointwise, not simultaneous confidence bands, and do not include geometric or material uncertainty.

ADAPT source and weights remain in the authors' repository. This page contains our adapter and derived inference outputs, with source/weight hashes in the reproducibility record. No independent experimental validation or inverse-design optimum is claimed.

2 · Minimum lift before a low-moment choice

Enter: Re = 3,000,000; α = 4°; minimum cₗ = 0.60; reference (x/c, z/c) = (0.35, 0).

Calculation: Clark Y lower lift screen = 0.618536 − 0.010791 = 0.607745, so it meets 0.60. NACA 2412 gives 0.597776 − 0.009226 = 0.588550, so it does not. Clark Y moment score = |−0.014378| + 0.002469 = 0.016847; NACA 4412 scores 0.021092. Clark Y therefore has the lowest score among the recorded sections passing this chosen screen. This is a fixed-angle screening result, not an optimized wing or an equal-lift trim comparison. No matching CFD case at 4° is supplied here.

3 · Moving a moment reference is not stiffening a wing

Enter: Clark Y, α = 4°, Re = 3,000,000. Compare x/c = 0.25 and 0.35, keeping z/c = 0.

Calculation: cₘ,new = cₘ,¼ + (x/c − 0.25)cₙ − (z/c)cₐ = −0.076235 + 0.10 × 0.618563 = −0.014378. Nose-up is positive. The moment changed because the reference point moved; EI, GJ, pressure and lift did not change. For structural use, compute the actual shear centre of the chosen construction in Wing Lab. Moment uncertainty uses the joint covariance, including cross-terms.
